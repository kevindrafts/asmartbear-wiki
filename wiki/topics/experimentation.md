# Experiments need a theory and room for surprise

Repeated testing can manufacture apparent wins when teams stop at favorable results or search many slices after the fact. Cohen asks for a hypothesis, a defined measure, and confirmation that important gains persist. A large-looking percentage from a small sample remains uncertain. [Repeated tests and false wins](https://longform.asmartbear.com/p-hacking/); [Patterns found after searching](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pattern-seeking-fallacy/); [Counts and uncertainty](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ab-testing-statistics/).

Disciplined measurement should not constrain every experiment to tiny variants. Incremental optimization can improve a weak starting point without finding a stronger proposition; distinctive product ideas may reveal value that the existing dashboard misses. [Testing a different direction](https://longform.asmartbear.com/local-minimum/); [Room for product taste](https://longform.asmartbear.com/creativity-over-optimization/).

**Editorial application:** Decide what customer belief is being tested, choose an effect that available traffic can meaningfully detect, and define review criteria before launch. Treat exploratory observations as hypotheses for fresh evidence. A failed test can teach something useful, while a favorable result needs the same scrutiny as an unwelcome one.
