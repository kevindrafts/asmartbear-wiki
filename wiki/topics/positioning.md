# Make a specific promise that the product fulfills

Positioning begins with a customer who prefers particular trade-offs. Cohen's target-market argument ties the promise to use and buying circumstances, while his strategic-choice framework requires accepting the costs of the position. Generic claims of quality or leadership cannot do that work. [The buyer's circumstances](https://longform.asmartbear.com/target-market/); [A coherent position](https://longform.asmartbear.com/strategic-choices/); [Empty category claims](https://longform.asmartbear.com/the-leading-provider/).

The promise must also survive contact with delivery. Comparing what the company emphasizes with what it actually does can reveal exaggerations, quiet strengths, and ordinary table stakes. A differentiated benefit helps only when buyers understand and experience it. [Claims and delivery](https://longform.asmartbear.com/talk-vs-walk/); [Observable proof](https://longform.asmartbear.com/show-dont-tell/).

**Editorial application:** State the recognizable problem, the specific benefit, and why this customer should believe it. Review the homepage, demo, sales materials, and support behavior together. If different customer groups experience the promise differently, split the analysis instead of hiding the mismatch in an average. A clearer statement can expose a strategy problem rather than merely solve a copywriting problem.
