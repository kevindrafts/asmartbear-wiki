# Sell an outcome through the real buying process

A convincing product benefit and an executable purchase are different requirements. Cohen's ROI experience shows how hypothetical labor savings may fail to release a tools budget. His purgatory model adds urgency: a buyer can honestly want something and still have more pressing priorities. [Budget reality](https://longform.asmartbear.com/roi-selling/); [Why interest does not close](https://longform.asmartbear.com/purgatory/).

The buyer and user may also differ. A tool that removes daily friction can acquire internal champions, and a small purchase can coexist with an incumbent rather than replace it. These routes do not eliminate enterprise procurement; they change the transaction you are attempting. [User-led adoption](https://longform.asmartbear.com/selling-to-the-bottom/); [Buying alongside an incumbent](https://longform.asmartbear.com/buy-both/).

**Editorial application:** Identify the user, sponsor, budget owner, approval path, alternative, and present trigger. Demonstrate the outcome in the customer's language. Negotiate terms with an understanding of delivery cost and trust, rather than assuming every objection means the list price must fall. [Consistent pricing rules](https://longform.asmartbear.com/discount-gambit/); [Terms that justify exceptional work](https://longform.asmartbear.com/say-yes/).
