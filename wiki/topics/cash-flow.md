# Timing can determine whether growth is affordable

Subscription revenue and spendable cash arrive on different schedules. Annual prepayment can bring receipts forward, but part of that cash belongs economically to future service delivery and potential refunds. Cohen's headline about an unlimited marketing budget is explicitly rhetorical. [Prepayment and delivery obligations](https://longform.asmartbear.com/annual-prepay/).

Growth itself can increase the cash requirement. Acquisition and onboarding costs may precede recurring receipts, while a shortfall in growth leaves already-committed costs intact. Long-run expected returns therefore do not settle the runway question. [Faster and slower growth scenarios](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-money/); [Payback and unstable lifetime estimates](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ltv/).

**Editorial application:** Model when cash is collected and spent, including service reserves, founder pay, overhead, and realistic acquisition payback. Compare annual and monthly plans without assuming annual buyers are more loyal because of the plan itself. Reconcile the billing system with bank movement so timing differences do not become hidden errors. [The unpaid-founder subsidy](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ramen-profitable/); [Independent reconciliation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/whos-lying/).
