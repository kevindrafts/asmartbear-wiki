# Choose a channel you can learn deeply

Cohen's first-channel rule combines three things: customers are present, the founder has relevant capability, and the work is sustainable enough to pursue seriously. Early focus helps a small team learn a medium instead of performing weakly everywhere. [Selecting the first channel](https://longform.asmartbear.com/first-marketing-channel/); [Depth before breadth](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-channel/).

Focus is not permanent dependence. Advertising results can change, a channel can saturate, and a platform can alter access or terms. Controlled experiments and a clear estimate of remaining reach help decide when to optimize the current channel or explore another. [Testing rather than guessing](https://longform.asmartbear.com/begin-advertising/); [Channel ceilings](https://longform.asmartbear.com/low-budget-marketing/); [Dependency risk](https://longform.asmartbear.com/marketing-platform-independence/).

**Editorial application:** Set a learning objective, an affordable exposure, a meaningful business outcome, and a review point. Consider whether you are competing for scarce inventory against larger budgets or building a distinct audience. The latter still requires effort and is not immune to algorithms. [Scarce slots and expandable audiences](https://longform.asmartbear.com/zero-sum-marketing/).

Treat historical platform examples as illustrations of selection, not a current list of universally effective channels.
