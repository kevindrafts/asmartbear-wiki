# Choose a business that fits the founder's real constraints

A persistent trait can help one strategy and hurt another. Cohen's Pivot Points approach starts with specific facts about capabilities, preferences, and constraints before labeling them strengths or weaknesses. His risk model then asks which customer and product are comparatively feasible for this team. [Facts before judgments](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pivot-points/); [Designing around weaknesses](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-drake-equation/).

Personal experience supplies insight, but does not make the founder representative of buyers. The person motivated to build an alternative may differ sharply from someone deciding whether switching is worth the trouble. Likewise, a favored marketing channel matters only if relevant customers use it. [Experience without projection](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scratched-my-own-itch/); [Capability meets audience](https://longform.asmartbear.com/first-marketing-channel/).

**Editorial application:** Describe what you can deliver consistently, what work you want to sustain, and what customers will pay for. Design the company around their intersection, then revisit it as the role changes. The [official self-discovery workshop](https://skills.asmartbear.com/workshops/find-yourself/) extends Cohen's exercises. A fit today is not a permanent identity or a promise of success.
