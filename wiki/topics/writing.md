# Write so the intended reader can recognize the point

Specific words help readers picture an action, audience, or consequence. Cohen's writing workshop replaces vague language with concrete details, while his critique of corporate descriptions shows how many words can still fail to identify a product. [Specificity in practice](https://longform.asmartbear.com/specificity/); [The cost of vague descriptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/the-leading-provider/).

Clarity must survive distraction. A pitch should convey a useful fragment during a short encounter, and a story should explain why the subject matters to its listener. Narrative power also creates a responsibility to check the explanation rather than let an engaging example stand in for proof. [Communication amid interruptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/standing-out-from-the-noise/); [Stories and evidence](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tell-me-a-story/).

**Editorial application:** Ask a reader to restate the message, replace unsupported adjectives with concrete evidence, and cut material that does not serve the purpose. Choose a list's length for what readers need to do, not because a familiar headline calls for ten items. [Purpose before list length](https://longform.asmartbear.com/top-10/).

Use invented examples transparently; specificity should increase accuracy, not manufacture it.
