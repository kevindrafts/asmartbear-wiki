# Interviews that change a decision

The purpose of discovery is to revise a consequential belief, not accumulate compliments. Cohen's later interview method starts with learning objectives and hypotheses, then asks about actual work and buying conditions. His earlier demand-validation essay is more emphatic about finding people who say they will buy; the later method makes the limitations of that evidence clearer. [Iterative customer development](https://longform.asmartbear.com/customer-development/); [Purchase intent](https://longform.asmartbear.com/customer-validation/).

Sample selection changes what you can learn. Existing customers explain useful outcomes, while people who abandoned a trial expose barriers that successful users already overcame. Support provides rich operational context but does not represent everyone who declined the product. [Lost sales](https://longform.asmartbear.com/put-down-the-compiler/); [Support conversations](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tech-support-is-sales/).

**Editorial application:** Write the decision the interview will inform, recruit people who experienced the relevant situation, record surprises, and distinguish observed behavior from hypothetical intent. A repeated request is evidence of a need, not an instruction to implement the customer's exact solution. Continue with the [official interview workshop](https://skills.asmartbear.com/workshops/customer-interviews/).

The [PainPointy interview preparation](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e08.md) shows the method being revised in conversation: separate the hypothesized role, segment, and outcome, then ask about current inputs and how ideas are judged. Cohen and Rison Simon have not yet collected the interview results. **Editorial connection:** A well-formed question improves the test; it does not validate the belief being tested. [E08 method](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/8/transcript#t=8m36s); [neutral prompts](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/8/transcript#t=46m49s).

In [Mindstamp](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e10.md), frequent support conversations leave important buying questions unanswered. Jessica Malnik proposes individual interviews with current and former customers before choosing adjacent markets and events. Cohen adds prospective customers and a test of whether content creation is actually blocking adoption. **Editorial connection:** Investigate a promising explanation before designing a service around it; an existing customer's lack of interest may also require testing a different audience. [E10 research sequence](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=1h0m25s); [Hypothesis and audience](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=1h2m27s).
