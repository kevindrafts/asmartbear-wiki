# Retention reveals both fit and growth limits

Customer losses matter as evidence about the product and as an economic burden. Cohen asks whether customers achieved the promised outcome, whether they were suitable buyers, and what changed before they left. Merely hearing the final cancellation reason may miss the earlier failure. [Diagnosing fit](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-market-fit-formula/); [Diagnosing stalled growth](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).

A constant loss rate acts on a growing installed base. Under fixed new monthly revenue, losses eventually match additions; better acquisition shifts that conditional ceiling, while stronger retention or expansion changes the underlying constraint. Replacement acquisition also consumes spending before net growth begins. [The revenue ceiling](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/); [Cost of cancellation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/coc/).

**Editorial application:** Segment retention by customer fit and cohort, investigate unsuccessful experiences against successful ones, and connect improvements to observable behavior. Avoid attributing a small aggregate churn change to one feature when delays and other causes make that claim unidentifiable. Contract duration can change collections without proving loyalty. [Attribution limits](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/); [Annual-payment selection effects](https://longform.asmartbear.com/annual-prepay/).
