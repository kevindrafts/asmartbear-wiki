# Retention reveals both fit and growth limits

Customer losses matter as evidence about the product and as an economic burden. Cohen asks whether customers achieved the promised outcome, whether they were suitable buyers, and what changed before they left. Merely hearing the final cancellation reason may miss the earlier failure. [Diagnosing fit](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-market-fit-formula/); [Diagnosing stalled growth](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).

A constant loss rate acts on a growing installed base. Under fixed new monthly revenue, losses eventually match additions; better acquisition shifts that conditional ceiling, while stronger retention or expansion changes the underlying constraint. Replacement acquisition also consumes spending before net growth begins. [The revenue ceiling](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/); [Cost of cancellation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/coc/).

**Editorial application:** Segment retention by customer fit and cohort, investigate unsuccessful experiences against successful ones, and connect improvements to observable behavior. Avoid attributing a small aggregate churn change to one feature when delays and other causes make that claim unidentifiable. Contract duration can change collections without proving loyalty. [Attribution limits](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/); [Annual-payment selection effects](https://longform.asmartbear.com/annual-prepay/).

In the [Podsqueeze discussion](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e03.md), Craig Hewitt distinguishes a hosting bill that can continue after a show ends from production software whose usefulness stops with new episodes. **Editorial connection:** Separate churn caused by an unsuccessful product experience from churn caused by the disappearance of the customer's job. The agency alternative discussed on the call is a hypothesis about a more persistent need, not a verified growth outcome. [E03](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/3/transcript#t=13m43s).

The [ScreenshotOne conversation](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e01.md) separates temporary projects from customers with an ongoing use. Cohen proposes an early-life cohort alongside longer-lived users so that expected project completion does not obscure recurring-use retention. **Editorial connection:** An aggregate cancellation rate can describe the growth constraint without explaining which customer relationship should change. [E01 segmentation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/1/transcript#t=29m29s).

The [Userlist discussion](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e11.md) distinguishes poor fit, incomplete implementation, and loss of an internal champion. Deicke limits the price-cancellation claim to a small recent sample; Cohen and Mariya Delano propose making results easier to share beyond one user. **Editorial connection:** Investigate who understands the value as well as who operates the product. Broader visibility is a proposed defense against organizational change, not a proven cure for churn. [E11 qualification and reporting](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=12m51s); [Delano's reporting suggestions](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=17m47s).
