# Choose customers by buying circumstances

A useful customer definition explains who values the product's trade-offs and why that person can buy now. Cohen's Carol is a decision aid, not an invented demographic biography. His later segmentation essay makes the same point through teams inside large companies: the buyer's workflow can matter more than the employer's size. [Ideal customer profile](https://longform.asmartbear.com/icp-ideal-customer-persona/); [Target market](https://longform.asmartbear.com/target-market/).

A segment also needs a reachable purchasing path. Enthusiasm without urgency leaves a product indefinitely postponed, while the broader market test asks whether the problem, budget, competition, and continued need fit together. Narrowing to a present buying trigger can be more productive than broadening the message. [Product purgatory](https://longform.asmartbear.com/purgatory/); [Problems and markets](https://longform.asmartbear.com/problem/).

**Editorial application:** Describe the buyer's situation, current alternative, trigger, constraints, and reason to prefer you. Test the description against wins, losses, and cancellations. Do not infer an enterprise strategy from a famous logo or treat the source's illustrative market multipliers as forecasts. Use the [official customer-profile workshop](https://skills.asmartbear.com/workshops/find-your-carol/) for Cohen's guided process.

The [PostPlanify conversation](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e05.md) makes the workflow boundary tangible: an agency creating content with client approval needs a different product from one where clients also do the authoring. Cohen and Laura Roeder help Hasan Cagli choose a direction; the call does not demonstrate its subsequent success. **Editorial connection:** Test an ICP through permissions, support, and daily work, not just the industry label. [E05 workflow](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/5/transcript#t=1h25m35s).

Two further conversations test whether a customer description is actionable. In [Ymir](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e06.md), the target becomes agencies whose teams already want AWS infrastructure and need help operating it. In [NaiL](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e07.md), Dan Martell suggests observable clues correlated with early technology adoption. **Editorial connection:** Separate a defining buying circumstance from an imperfect signal used to find prospects; validate the latter instead of turning it into a universal filter. [E06 target](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=1h24m49s); [E07 signals](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/7/transcript#t=24m42s).

The [Mindstamp discussion](../../processed/smart-bear-live-e10.md) separates a customizable product from an overly broad audience. A narrow buyer may value flexibility, but a curious prospect without content or an approved project still cannot adopt it. **Editorial connection:** Describe both the reason to prefer the product and the circumstances that make implementation possible. [E10 flexibility](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=7m48s); [Project readiness](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=26m22s).
