# Distribution needs a mechanism, not a channel list

Knowing how to build the product does not establish how buyers will find it. Cohen criticizes plans that merely name advertising, social media, affiliates, and A/B testing. A useful plan explains why an audience pays attention and how that attention becomes relevant demand. [Beyond a list of tactics](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-marketing-ideas/); [Testing commercial reach](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-weekend/).

Different mechanisms carry different constraints. Built-in invitations expose new people to product value; authority can open trusted conversations; paid acquisition needs viable economics. A partnership also has to matter to the partner, whose reputation and operational effort are real costs. [Sharing and acquisition](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-marketing-ideas/); [The partner's business model](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-biz-dev/).

**Editorial application:** Draw the sequence from a buyer's existing activity to discovery, evaluation, and purchase. Identify the step with evidence and the step still assumed. Favor qualified conversations over vanity attention, and follow up with context after in-person encounters. [Learning from trade shows](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tradeshow-tips-checklist/).

A successful distribution experiment is an input to a business model, not permission to ignore service, retention, or the channel's eventual ceiling.
