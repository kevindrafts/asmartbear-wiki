# Make a decision process fit the decision

The right amount of deliberation depends on reversibility, stakes, uncertainty, and the speed of feedback. Cohen favors quick learning for many choices while reserving deeper thought for commitments that are hard to undo. He also distinguishes seeking an acceptable option from seeking the best available one. [Fast and slow decisions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/decisions-fast-slow/); [Sufficiency and excellence](https://longform.asmartbear.com/maximizing/).

For consequential choices, clarify the outcome before combining scores. A weakness may disqualify an otherwise strong option, while ordered priorities can distinguish acceptable options without pretending unlike benefits share a precise unit. Genuine ties can be broken by an explicitly secondary preference. [Separating gains and liabilities](https://longform.asmartbear.com/complex-decisions/); [Binstack](https://longform.asmartbear.com/maximized-decision/); [A viable tie](https://longform.asmartbear.com/business-tiebreaker/).

**Editorial application:** State the decision, criteria, feasible alternatives, accepted downside, and what new evidence would change the choice. Do not confuse willingness to revise with changing every time someone offers an opinion; the evidence behind the existing decision matters. [Proportionate scrutiny](https://longform.asmartbear.com/strong-opinions-weakly-held/).
