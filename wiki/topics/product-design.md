# Design as a chosen source of customer preference

Cohen's older redesign story reports improved impressions without an obvious change in the displayed conversion measures. Its later update acknowledges rising expectations for business-software design. The lesson is to locate design in the actual strategy, rather than infer either universal importance or irrelevance from one chart. [Design and changing expectations](https://longform.asmartbear.com/design/).

A distinctive experience can also generate preference that incremental optimization misses. In the SLC approach, a narrow first release should provide a complete outcome with some reason to enjoy it; that reason may be service or identity as well as visual polish. [Product taste](https://longform.asmartbear.com/creativity-over-optimization/); [A lovable first product](https://longform.asmartbear.com/slc/).

**Editorial application:** Name the customer experience design must improve, maintain essential usability, and test whether the selected distinction matters. Do not require a measurable revenue effect for each tiny change, but do not assume every expensive aesthetic choice is valuable either. Quality often emerges from many small improvements whose individual effects are difficult to isolate. [The collective value of small improvements](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rocks-pebbles-sand/).
