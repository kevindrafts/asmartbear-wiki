# Choose work by the kind of impact it must create

Prioritization allocates finite capacity and leaves real needs unresolved. Cohen asks leaders to name both the consequential outcome and the deferrals, because unexplained omissions look like indifference to people affected by them. [Making exclusions explicit](https://longform.asmartbear.com/prioritization/).

One ranking formula does not fit all work. Strategic commitments need enough absolute impact to justify their duration; tactical improvements can be compared by benefit relative to effort; small maintenance needs low coordination overhead. Detailed ratios can manufacture distinctions smaller than the estimation error. [Different work, different rules](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rocks-pebbles-sand/); [Honest scoring bands](https://longform.asmartbear.com/roi-rubric/).

**Editorial application:** Separate mandatory obligations, major outcomes, tactical improvements, and routine upkeep. Preserve stakeholder-specific input streams, then choose the next executable work using strategy, capacity, and dependencies. Separate lists organize evidence but do not make the final allocation automatic. [Independent streams, one plan](https://longform.asmartbear.com/jit-backlogs/).

If none of the major ideas clears the impact threshold, improve the ideas rather than committing months to the least disappointing option.
