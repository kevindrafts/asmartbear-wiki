# Scale changes the operating problem

A larger company is not a small company with proportional staffing. Rare incidents become common in aggregate, dependencies multiply, and continuity requires redundancy. Cohen shows why coordination and specialist capabilities can reduce individual output while improving reliable organizational performance. [The forces of scale](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/); [Frequent exceptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale-rare/).

Growth also changes lead times and the size of a worthwhile initiative. Hiring, training, launches, and service capacity must align; an opportunity too small to move a large company's results may still be excellent for a startup. These differences explain both the costs and advantages of scale. [Predictability and materiality](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/); [The startup opportunity](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-beats-incumbent/).

**Editorial application:** Identify which informal practices have become brittle, then invest in the specific systems or expertise required. Protect slack for normal variation, and avoid treating every delay as bureaucracy or every process as necessary maturity. [Capacity under variation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/utilization/).

Preserve values while adapting roles and methods. A founder's early strengths may no longer match the daily work required at the next stage.
