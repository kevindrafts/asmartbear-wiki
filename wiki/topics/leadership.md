# Create clarity and ownership under uncertainty

Being in command does not mean preventing every surprise. Cohen describes a leader who understands important deviations, prioritizes responses, and communicates what is changing. A first-time manager's contribution becomes building a capable team rather than remaining the best individual problem-solver. [Accountability without omniscience](https://longform.asmartbear.com/in-command/); [The management transition](https://longform.asmartbear.com/advice-first-time-manager/).

Autonomy needs shared direction. Clear strategic choices reduce inconsistent local decisions, while a current plan connects goals to obstacles, action, and evidence. Teams should understand both what matters and which worthwhile requests will wait. [Alignment through choices](https://longform.asmartbear.com/strategic-choices/); [An understandable plan](https://longform.asmartbear.com/strategic-planning/).

**Editorial application:** Make ownership, expectations, dependencies, and review points visible. Ask whether repeated intervention indicates a capability gap, unclear context, or the manager's reluctance to release control. Examine motivation through the actual role and the person's goals before assuming a motivational speech will fix a mismatch. [Role and motivation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/how-to-motivate-an-employee/).

Neither empowerment nor accountability requires pretending uncertainty has disappeared.
