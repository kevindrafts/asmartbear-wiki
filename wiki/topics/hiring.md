# Hire the capability the next stage needs

A first employee needs to function in a different environment from a specialist joining a large organization. Cohen's early hiring advice emphasizes communication and ambiguity; his scaling essay explains why later candidates may reasonably want clearer roles, reliable pay, and larger problems. [Early hiring](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-hiring-advice/); [Different stages and motivations](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/).

Hiring beyond the founder's expertise requires more than a keyword check. Discussion of real problems, evidence of direct experience, and references about the environment in which someone succeeds can make evaluation more useful. Product roles particularly benefit from mapping complementary capabilities instead of searching for a person excellent at everything. [Evaluating stronger specialists](https://longform.asmartbear.com/hire-better-than-you/); [Coverage across a team](https://longform.asmartbear.com/great-product-manager/).

**Editorial application:** Define the outcome and missing capability, describe actual working conditions, and use consistent job-relevant evidence. Include recruiting and ramp time in the capacity plan. Personal chemistry can aid collaboration, but shared humor or resemblance to the founder is not a substitute for competence or fair evaluation.
