# Keep judgment honest about what is unknown

A confidence score can look quantitative without being defined or calibrated. Cohen prefers identifying what can be observed, bounding exposure, and choosing investments whose potential benefit justifies survivable losses. Neither a precise-looking score nor personal conviction creates evidence. [The confidence problem](https://longform.asmartbear.com/confidence/).

Success stories add another trap: the observed winners may share the same practices as unseen failures. Crowds can help with factual estimation when errors offset, but correlated beliefs and preference compromises can produce a different result. [Missing cases](https://longform.asmartbear.com/survivor-bias/); [When aggregation helps](https://longform.asmartbear.com/wisdom-of-crowds/).

**Editorial application:** Separate known constraints, estimated quantities, and unresolved assumptions. Seek contradictory cases, independent measurements, and affordable ways to learn. A single event cannot validate or invalidate its assigned probability; some causal contributions cannot be identified even afterward. [Limits of attribution](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/); [Independent checks](https://longform.asmartbear.com/whos-lying/).
