# Create alternatives beyond today's optimization

Cohen's extreme questions deliberately distort price, resources, time, or service assumptions to generate ideas outside a familiar roadmap. The value is in translating a surprising response into a feasible experiment, not implementing the extreme literally. [Breaking habitual assumptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/extreme-questions/).

Creative choices also differ from factual voting. Accommodating every preference can remove the distinctive benefit that would attract a particular audience. At the same time, a strong personal idea needs exposure to use and purchase behavior so it can evolve. [The limits of consensus design](https://longform.asmartbear.com/wisdom-of-crowds/); [Discovery through change](https://longform.asmartbear.com/your-idea-sucks-do-it-anyway/).

**Editorial application:** Generate alternatives independently, choose the customer benefit each pursues, and test the important uncertainty. New technology is not itself a market or a durable defense. Cohen's AI essay specifically challenges the assumption that incumbents will ignore the same enabling technology available to startups. [AI and incumbent advantages](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ai-startups/).

Treat that essay as a dated strategic assessment. Evaluate the actual problem, distribution, data, reliability, and differentiation rather than importing a universal verdict about a fast-changing technology.
