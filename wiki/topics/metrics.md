# Measure the system without mistaking a proxy for the goal

Cohen's earlier one-priority story uses a decisive growth objective to focus a young company. His later product-metrics framework explicitly broadens that view: work, usage, operational health, and downstream outcomes differ in timing and control. This is an evolution in the corpus, not one timeless rule. [The early focus argument](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-priority/); [The later KPI framework](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-metrics/).

Different functions also speak different operational languages. A product team must explain its business contribution without pretending revenue depends on that team alone. A useful measure has a definition, owner, cadence, and consequence, and some measures need maintenance thresholds rather than endless maximization. [Translating contributions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/three-languages/); [Measures and standards](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-metrics/).

**Editorial application:** Trace each dashboard item to the decision it informs. Keep leading evidence and lagging outcomes together, validate instrumentation independently, and acknowledge attribution limits. The article that argues for several incompatible supreme SaaS metrics is intentionally rhetorical: circumstance determines the useful priority. [Checking the instruments](https://longform.asmartbear.com/whos-lying/); [No universal supreme metric](https://longform.asmartbear.com/saas-metric/).
