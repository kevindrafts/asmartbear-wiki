# Product–market fit is sustained customer pull

Cohen looks for a combination of acquisition, meaningful use, and retention. A busy funnel can conceal a product people abandon; operational overload can also accompany real demand. His selected growth charts describe a changed trajectory rather than proving that fit automatically produces exponential growth. [Experience and data](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-market-fit/).

The route is less uniform than the destination. WP Engine's discovery-led path and Smart Bear's evolving product direction support different learning sequences. A useful framework identifies the present milestone and obstacle without pretending that interviews, shipping, and pivots must occur in one universal order. [The roadmap and its exceptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/product-market-fit-formula/).

**Editorial application:** Look at cohorts and buying behavior together. Determine whether losses arise from broken promises, unsuitable customers, or a need that naturally ends. Then ask whether the business can acquire and serve customers sustainably. The thresholds in these essays are Cohen's practical definitions, not an industry certification. A stable, profitable niche can be a worthwhile outcome even without the growth path its founder first imagined. [Plateaus and owner goals](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).
