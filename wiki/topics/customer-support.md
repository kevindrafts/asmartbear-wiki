# Support as delivery, learning, and trust

Support is where a company's promise meets a customer's actual work. Cohen argues for helping the customer reach an outcome rather than merely answering the literal question. The interaction can improve retention, advocacy, and sales while revealing a product misunderstanding. [Support's wider contribution](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tech-support-is-sales/).

That promise must fit the operating model. A side project cannot credibly offer the availability of a staffed service. At scale, rare incidents become frequent across the company, and notification, escalation, and specialist work grow along with technical complexity. [Honest service constraints](https://longform.asmartbear.com/working-startup/); [Incident consequences at scale](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale-rare/).

**Editorial application:** Match advertised responsiveness to actual capacity, empower people to resolve appropriate issues, and review recurring confusion for product changes. Distinguish support's immediate resolution measure from longer-term customer success. Observable service behavior also makes brand claims credible. [Proving the promise](https://longform.asmartbear.com/show-dont-tell/).

Support data is rich but selected: silent abandoners and non-buyers need other research methods. Exceptional service can be an advantage without being the correct cost structure for every product.
