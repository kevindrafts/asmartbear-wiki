# Compete where your difference matters

Startups rarely win by reproducing every advantage of an incumbent. Cohen identifies constraints created by those very advantages: a large installed base resists disruptive changes, while a large cost structure makes small opportunities unattractive. A narrow customer group may prefer a simpler product and direct attention. [Incumbent constraints](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-beats-incumbent/).

The relevant comparison is therefore customer-specific. Being weaker overall can coexist with being distinctly better at the job that matters to your segment. Being first, by contrast, is only a historical fact unless it becomes an experienced benefit or a durable capability. [Different trade-offs](https://longform.asmartbear.com/worse-but-unique/); [The limits of first entry](https://longform.asmartbear.com/first-competitive-advantage/).

**Editorial application:** Identify the alternative customers actually use, including doing nothing. Explain why your chosen advantage is valuable and why the rival would struggle to follow. Limit speculation about private competitor plans, and do not copy a feature merely because another company displays it. [The second-guessing trap](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rock-paper-scissors/); [Features and unknown effects](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/).
