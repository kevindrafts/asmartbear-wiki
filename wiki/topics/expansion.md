# Expand without dismantling the working business

Expansion after product–market fit can reuse existing strengths, but superficial similarity hides functional change. Cohen's adjacency analysis checks marketing, sales, service, product, engineering, and economics separately. Selling similar software to a different buyer can still require a new business system. [The adjacency matrix](https://longform.asmartbear.com/adjacency/).

A large customer's purchase does not necessarily signal an enterprise opportunity. The buyer may be a single team with the same job as your smaller customers. Conversely, larger organizational deals can demand predictability, specialist service, and controls absent from the original model. [Who actually bought](https://longform.asmartbear.com/target-market/); [Operating at scale](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/).

**Editorial application:** Identify the capability you can reuse, the buyer whose needs genuinely overlap, and every function that must change. Distinguish a manageable extension from several simultaneous rebuilds. A feasibility map does not establish market attractiveness; combine it with evidence of demand and an honest assessment of whether more growth serves the owner's goals. [Expansion and the choice to grow](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).
