# Forecast conditionally and learn from recorded predictions

Cohen distinguishes growth shapes that headlines often collapse into exponential growth. Saturating channels, changing market share, and pricing can produce different aggregate paths. A good fit to historical data does not establish the mechanism or guarantee its continuation. [Growth curves and components](https://longform.asmartbear.com/exponential-growth/).

Operational planning still needs expectations. Revenue scenarios inform staffing and cash commitments, while alternative paths protect against a single mistaken prediction. At larger scale, rejecting all predictability can harm coordinated execution. [Cash scenarios](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-money/); [Robustness under uncertainty](https://longform.asmartbear.com/predict-the-future/); [Why planning becomes necessary](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/).

**Editorial application:** State assumptions, ranges, dependencies, and the observation that would trigger a revision. Save probabilistic forecasts before outcomes occur and evaluate repeated comparable cases against a baseline, considering both calibration and discrimination. The worked numbers in the forecasting article contain inconsistencies, so reproduce its conceptual lesson with independently checked arithmetic. [Evaluating forecast accuracy](https://longform.asmartbear.com/forecast/).

A conditional revenue ceiling is a useful warning signal; it is not a prediction that its inputs remain fixed. [A conditional equilibrium](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/).
