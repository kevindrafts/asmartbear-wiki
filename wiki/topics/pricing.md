# Price the business you can actually deliver

Price helps determine the customer, acquisition process, service level, and cost structure. Cohen therefore treats it as part of business design rather than a final markup applied to finished software. Equal revenue from many small buyers or a few large ones creates different demands and financing possibilities. [Pricing and the business model](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pricing-determines-your-business-model/); [Price versus customer count](https://longform.asmartbear.com/price-vs-quantity/).

Higher is not always better. Premium excellence, practical value, and affordable access are distinct positions. A low price can work when deliberate operating choices sustain it; reactive discounting on an otherwise undifferentiated product is a different proposition. [Three price–value positions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-or-less/); [When low price is coherent](https://longform.asmartbear.com/never-compete-on-price/).

Customers' motivation also matters. Buying because the product is useful or beloved differs from paying because leaving is painful. A value calculation cannot substitute for budget access, purchasing authority, and a credible improvement to work. [Motives for paying](https://longform.asmartbear.com/willingness-to-pay/); [The limits of ROI selling](https://longform.asmartbear.com/roi-selling/).

**Editorial application:** Test price with the intended segment and delivery model, including acquisition and support costs. Treat historical price bands as examples and preserve trust when changing terms. The [official skills site](https://skills.asmartbear.com/) links the author's pricing tools.
