# Focus creates capacity by ending commitments

Cohen treats focus as subtraction: stopping activities releases time for the few outcomes that matter. A list of priorities that leaves every obligation intact is only a statement of preference. [Creating space](https://longform.asmartbear.com/focus/); [Accepting the cost of deferral](https://longform.asmartbear.com/prioritization/).

The number of priorities follows the task. One company-wide outcome may guide an early team, while a complete audit requires every item and a larger organization needs distinct responsibilities. A fixed list length is a formatting habit, not a decision method. [An early-stage focus episode](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-priority/); [The purpose of a list](https://longform.asmartbear.com/top-10/).

**Editorial application:** For the next priority, name what stops, who is affected, and when the choice will be reviewed. Protect concentrated work and capacity for variation. A narrow focus is compatible with maintaining essential standards; it is not an excuse to neglect obligations or to keep pursuing a project because of its past cost. [Attention and interruption](https://longform.asmartbear.com/stop-chopping-to-pieces/); [Reassessing continuation](https://longform.asmartbear.com/sunk-costs/).
