# When should I hire, and how do I become a manager?

**Use this when:** The founder is a recurring bottleneck, the team lacks a critical capability, or demand will soon exceed reliable capacity.

1. **Diagnose the work problem.** Distinguish unnecessary work, poor coordination, and a genuine capability or capacity shortage. A permanently full schedule is not proof of efficient staffing. [Capacity and interruptions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/utilization/).
2. **Define the outcome to own.** Specify the missing result and authority needed to achieve it. Hiring someone merely to copy the founder's method limits the benefit. [Build capability beyond yourself](https://longform.asmartbear.com/delegation/).
3. **Plan backward from readiness.** Recruiting, notice periods, training, and ramp-up take time. A growing service organization must anticipate demand, while a small company must also preserve cash. [Hiring lead times](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/); [Coverage and management](https://longform.asmartbear.com/advice-first-time-manager/).
4. **Assess relevant evidence.** Discuss real problems, ask how the candidate reasons, and use references to understand context. Describe actual working conditions and evaluate communication consistently. [Hiring stronger specialists](https://longform.asmartbear.com/hire-better-than-you/); [Early-team fit](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-hiring-advice/).
5. **Change your own job.** Provide direction, boundaries, feedback, and a way to see results. Let the new owner improve the method instead of requiring permission for every step. [Accountable leadership](https://longform.asmartbear.com/in-command/).

**Leave with:** A justified role, affordable capacity plan, evaluation criteria, and an ownership transition. Personal likeness is not competence, and empowerment does not mean abandoning management. If the problem is an unrealistic scope or promise, hiring alone may only make it more expensive.
