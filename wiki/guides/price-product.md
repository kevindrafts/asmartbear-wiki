# How should I price this product or service?

**Use this when:** Choosing an initial price, reconsidering tiers, or reacting to pressure for discounts. This guide supports business reasoning; historical source prices are not current benchmarks.

1. **Choose the business shape.** Specify the customer, purchase process, service promise, and scale you want. A price that requires personal selling must support its cost. Many small accounts and a few large accounts are different operating systems. [Design price with the business](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pricing-determines-your-business-model/); [Compare customer models](https://longform.asmartbear.com/price-vs-quantity/).
2. **Define the position.** Decide whether customers choose premium excellence, practical value, or affordable access. Explain the cost structure and deliberate limitations that sustain the choice. [Price–value positions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-or-less/); [Coherent low-cost strategy](https://longform.asmartbear.com/never-compete-on-price/).
3. **Investigate the purchase.** Identify the budget owner, alternatives, approval constraints, and outcome the buyer values. Hypothetical time savings do not necessarily release a budget. [The purchasing reality](https://longform.asmartbear.com/roi-selling/).
4. **Model delivery honestly.** Include support, acquisition, overhead, and your own labor. For services, distinguish paid hours from total employed capacity; for subscriptions, inspect margin, retention, and payback separately. [Billable capacity](https://longform.asmartbear.com/consulting-company-accounting/); [Component economics](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ltv/).
5. **Test and review.** Use concrete offers and customer conversations to challenge assumptions, while considering trust and existing commitments. Establish understandable terms before an improvised concession becomes your policy. [The cost of arbitrary discounting](https://longform.asmartbear.com/discount-gambit/).

**Leave with:** A price hypothesis, an economically plausible delivery model, evidence from the intended buyer, and a review trigger. A price rise can fail, and an attractive value calculation is not proof of willingness to pay. The [official skills site](https://skills.asmartbear.com/) provides the author's pricing tools.
