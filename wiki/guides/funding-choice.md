# Should I bootstrap or raise money?

**Use this when:** Both funding paths appear possible, or capital is being proposed as the answer to an unclear bottleneck.

1. **Define the desired company.** Describe owner income, autonomy, growth ambition, and the daily work each path creates. A valuable niche can suit a bootstrapper without meeting venture return expectations. [Different contests](https://longform.asmartbear.com/bootstrapped-beats-vc/); [Choosing the experience](https://longform.asmartbear.com/business-tiebreaker/).
2. **Name what capital buys.** Distinguish expanding a demonstrated mechanism from paying to search for one. Show evidence of learning and the concrete resources a milestone requires. [Capital as an accelerant](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-money/).
3. **Model the cash paths.** Include founder compensation, service costs, acquisition timing, and cases where growth is faster or slower than expected. [Honest sustainability](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ramen-profitable/); [Both sides of the cash surprise](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-money/).
4. **Check structural fit.** Compare the price, customer count, sales process, and hiring required by each option. Do not depend on implausible future improvements to make either case work. [The operating model behind revenue](https://longform.asmartbear.com/price-vs-quantity/).
5. **Make the trade-off explicit.** If both paths remain credible, preference is a legitimate tie-breaker. If neither does, more analysis of investor fashion will not fix the business assumption. [A decision after sufficient analysis](https://longform.asmartbear.com/business-tiebreaker/).

**Leave with:** A chosen financing hypothesis, milestones, downside cases, and a reason it fits the intended life and company. This guide does not prescribe transaction terms or provide investment advice; the articles supply conceptual comparisons rather than current financing benchmarks.
