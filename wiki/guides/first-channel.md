# Which marketing channel should I try first?

**Use this when:** You have a specific buyer hypothesis but no repeatable way to reach those people.

1. **Locate the actual audience.** Identify where buyers already learn, compare alternatives, or ask for help. Do not choose a channel solely because other founders recommend it. [Audience and founder fit](https://longform.asmartbear.com/first-marketing-channel/).
2. **Choose a mechanism you can sustain.** Explain why someone would notice, engage, and take the next step. Content, direct conversations, product invitations, and advertising each need more than a platform account. [A mechanism beyond tactics](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-marketing-ideas/).
3. **Concentrate the initial effort.** Learn one promising channel deeply enough to judge it. Preserve alternative ideas without attempting all of them simultaneously. [Early concentration](https://longform.asmartbear.com/one-channel/); [Options without simultaneous execution](https://longform.asmartbear.com/capturing-luck/).
4. **Bound the experiment.** Select an affordable test with timely feedback and a meaningful business outcome. Include the effort involved, not only the advertising bill. [A measurable channel test](https://longform.asmartbear.com/begin-advertising/).
5. **Review quality and ceiling.** Evaluate qualified demand and conversion, then estimate remaining inventory. If the channel works but is nearly exhausted, expansion may matter more than another small optimization. [The next use of a small budget](https://longform.asmartbear.com/low-budget-marketing/).

**Leave with:** A customer–channel hypothesis, a concrete message, a budget or time limit, a success criterion, and a review date. A channel that works today can change. Once there is something worth protecting, examine dependence on its platform and develop a feasible fallback. [Dependency review](https://longform.asmartbear.com/marketing-platform-independence/).
