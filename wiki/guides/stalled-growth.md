# Why has growth stalled?

**Use this when:** New customers still arrive but recurring revenue grows slowly, or a once-effective growth approach stops improving results.

1. **Verify the picture.** Reconcile billing, customer counts, and cash timing. Check definitions and instrumentation before interpreting a change. [Check independent evidence](https://longform.asmartbear.com/whos-lying/).
2. **Separate additions from losses.** Inspect cohorts, cancellations, downgrades, and expansion. With fixed new MRR and a constant positive revenue-loss rate, growth approaches a ceiling; that conditional model explains a plateau without assuming demand vanished. [The growth ceiling](https://longform.asmartbear.com/max-mrr/).
3. **Find the binding constraint.** Cohen's plateau sequence examines retention, pricing, account expansion, acquisition, and market limits. Investigate failed customer outcomes before paying to replace more disappointed users. [Diagnose the mechanism](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).
4. **Examine channel capacity.** An optimized campaign can still run out of reachable inventory. Compare improving shared conversion steps with developing another channel, allowing time for discovery. [Optimize or expand distribution](https://longform.asmartbear.com/low-budget-marketing/); [Saturating growth components](https://longform.asmartbear.com/exponential-growth/).
5. **Check the desired destination.** Further expansion may require different customers, capabilities, and cash. Compare that investment with a profitable steady business or a changed owner role. [Expansion requirements](https://longform.asmartbear.com/adjacency/); [The choice about further growth](https://longform.asmartbear.com/growth-asymptote/).

**Leave with:** A diagnosed constraint, one consequential intervention, supporting measures, and a review point. Do not treat churn thresholds as universal or attribute every small retention change to one feature. [Attribution limits](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/).
