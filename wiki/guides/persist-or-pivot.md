# Should I keep going, pivot, or stop?

**Use this when:** Progress is weak, prior investment feels hard to abandon, and the next commitment needs a defensible reason.

1. **Separate past investment from future value.** Ask what continuing would cost from today and what evidence supports its benefit. Include real transition costs, but do not treat unrecoverable effort as a reason to spend more. [Reassess the next decision](https://longform.asmartbear.com/sunk-costs/).
2. **Identify what is failing.** Is the problem demand, urgency, distribution, economics, delivery, or team capacity? A broad feeling that the startup is difficult does not isolate the assumption to test. [The chain of required conditions](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-drake-equation/).
3. **Look for actual pull elsewhere.** Unexpected use or buyer interest can reveal a better direction. Successful pivot stories establish possibility, not a guarantee that persistence will eventually pay. [Learning from the attempt](https://longform.asmartbear.com/your-idea-sucks-do-it-anyway/).
4. **Bound the next experiment.** Choose a material uncertainty, a tolerable exposure, and an observation that will change the decision. Do not keep redefining success after each result. [A survivable commitment](https://longform.asmartbear.com/confidence/); [Avoid favorable-result chasing](https://longform.asmartbear.com/p-hacking/).
5. **Include personal fit.** Consider whether the work and responsibility remain worthwhile and sustainable, including the effect on people around you. Confidence need not be constant, but distress is not proof that the business should continue. [The venture and the founder](https://longform.asmartbear.com/emotional-rollercoaster/).

**Leave with:** A decision or a bounded learning step, an owner, a review point, and explicit continuation criteria. No retrospective success story can tell you with certainty which path this venture will take.
