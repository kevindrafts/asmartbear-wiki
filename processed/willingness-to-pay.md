# Willingness-to-pay: Creating permanent competitive advantage for the right reasons

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/willingness-to-pay/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Payment can reflect affection, practical usefulness, or an absence of tolerable alternatives. Cohen argues that these motivations have different strategic consequences even when they produce the same immediate revenue: enthusiastic customers may advocate, useful tools earn continued use, and resentful customers search for escape. His value diagram separates customer benefit from price and delivery cost, encouraging companies to create more value before deciding how to share it.

**Apply it:** Investigate why customers stay and what they would choose if switching were easy. Improve the experience or outcome behind genuine preference instead of relying solely on exit barriers.

**Boundary:** These categories overlap and are hard to measure directly. Historical company examples are interpretive; stable profit margins alone cannot establish how willingness to pay changed, and customer affection never guarantees retention.
