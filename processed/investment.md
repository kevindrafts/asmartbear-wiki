# Deciding whether an investment is worthwhile

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/investment/) · Unofficial editorial note.

For commitments consuming years of life, Cohen argues that an average monetary payoff hides too much: the person experiences one path, not an entire portfolio. He favors substantial possible upside, value that accumulates, durable demand or strong trends, a personal execution advantage, and alignment with long-term goals.

**Apply it:** Evaluate a major commitment against all five conditions and make the downside survivable; use rough magnitude estimates without pretending uncertain probabilities are precise.

**Boundary:** This is Cohen's personal decision framework, not financial advice. Expected value can still inform diversified repeated bets, and his illustrative earnings arithmetic should not be treated as a rigorous career-return calculation.
