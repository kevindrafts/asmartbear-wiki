# Worrying is self-fulfilling; what to do instead

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/worry/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen distinguishes rehearsing feared outcomes from preparation that changes what happens next. Sports and performance examples introduce the idea that useful planning precedes action, while repetitive self-questioning can interfere with execution. He applies the distinction to payroll, competition, sales, and doubts about competence.

**Apply it:** Convert a concern into a specific preparatory action or decision, then give the immediate task your attention instead of continually replaying the concern.

**Boundary:** This is a motivational essay, not clinical guidance. Anxiety is not always voluntary, and worrying does not make someone responsible for the adverse event they fear.
