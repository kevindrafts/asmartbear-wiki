# On the value of Judgement

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/judgement/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Avoiding judgment altogether prevents prioritization and commitment. Cohen distinguishes openness to unfamiliar perspectives from treating every idea as equally valid. He argues for improving the skills behind judgment—recognizing bias, identifying what matters, combining evidence with vision—so a team can choose and act.

**Apply it:** Make the criteria behind a consequential choice explicit and invite challenges to those criteria before committing.

**Boundary:** Choosing a direction does not establish universal truth. The essay's defense of judgment remains compatible with revising a decision when better evidence arrives.
