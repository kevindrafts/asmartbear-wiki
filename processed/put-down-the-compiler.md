# Put down the compiler until you learn why they’re not buying

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/put-down-the-compiler/) · Unofficial editorial note.

When people try a young product but do not buy, Cohen urges technical founders to investigate those lost sales before building another feature release. Existing customers reveal what satisfied buyers value, but they have already overcome the barriers that defeated non-buyers. Familiarity also makes founders poor proxies for a novice confronting an empty screen or failed installation.

**Apply it:** Contact recent trial abandoners, observe their first-use experience, and identify which promised outcome they could not reach. Let that evidence determine whether code, onboarding, positioning, or customer selection needs attention.

**Boundary:** This argument addresses an early product with enough functionality to test demand. Its dismissive characterization of users is unnecessary to the useful point: expertise creates blind spots, and missing features remain a hypothesis to investigate.
