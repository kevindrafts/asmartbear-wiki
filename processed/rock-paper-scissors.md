# But I know that he knows that I know

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rock-paper-scissors/) · Unofficial editorial note.

A playful analysis of rock–paper–scissors shows how a supposedly winning tactic depends on what an opponent knows about that tactic. Each additional layer of anticipated reaction can reverse the recommendation. Cohen uses the resulting regress to question founders who spend their attention guessing competitors' private plans.

**Apply it:** Distinguish observable competitor behavior from assumptions about motives, and redirect speculative analysis toward a product customers actually prefer.

**Boundary:** The game and accompanying strategy illustration are analogies, not a validated competitive framework. The useful lesson is to limit unsupported second-guessing; it does not eliminate the need to understand alternatives customers can buy.
