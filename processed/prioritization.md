# Ruthless prioritization while the dog pees on the floor

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/prioritization/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen treats prioritization as an unavoidable allocation of finite time, including the decision to leave legitimate problems unresolved. His high-impact versus low-impact framing encourages founders to seek consequential work, eliminate avoidable obligations, and delegate or batch the rest. The neglected-dog story explains why a reasonable allocation can look negligent to someone who lacks the context.

**Apply it:** Name the decisive outcome, the work that advances it, and the issues deliberately deferred. Explain the costs of deferral repeatedly so people can judge the choice rather than infer indifference.

**Boundary:** Effort and impact are different: many small design details can collectively create a major advantage. The binary framing is intentionally reductive, and the article offers size-based frameworks when intermediate work matters. It is not permission to ignore every inconvenient obligation.
