# How many things should there be? (Hint: Not 10)

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/top-10/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen questions lists whose length comes from a familiar format instead of their purpose. A single priority, one item per team, and a complete audit checklist have different reasons for their size. An enormous stale backlog may need archiving rather than another round of ranking.

**Apply it:** Decide what the list must help someone do, then choose its length and structure accordingly. Preserve records when clearing a backlog so a genuine need can be recovered.

**Boundary:** The numerical examples are illustrations, not universal cognitive limits; the article explicitly labels its post-COVID memory claim unsupported. Some lists require completeness, so shorter is not automatically better.
