# E14 w/ Ben Orenstein: How to Price Your SaaS for Long-Term Growth

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Ben Orenstein, Chris Oliver.

Hatchbox helps deploy and maintain applications on customers' own infrastructure. Chris Oliver wants it to support a small team as his education business declines. Cohen and Orenstein examine whether the customer, service promise, acquisition method, and founder's preferred work can fit together. [Conversation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript).

- **Price the responsibility the customer expects.** Cohen identifies a mismatch between low fees and expectations of continuous availability and urgent assistance, even though Hatchbox does not earn infrastructure revenue. He proposes either charging enough to fund the service or offering a more limited response commitment. His example prices and response times illustrate segmentation; they are not validated packages. [Operating burden](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=50m36s); [Proposed service boundaries](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=1h5m13s).
- **Fit the strategy to the founder while changing what no longer works.** Orenstein questions a plan that requires Oliver to spend his days doing work he dislikes. They explore renewed educational content, a different product position, and agency sales. Cohen cautions that a small team cannot simultaneously rebuild its platform and transform how it sells. Oliver later explains why making sponsored content alone would leave out building work he needs. No final direction is selected. [Founder fit](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=16m44s); [Transformation capacity](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=32m42s); [Content-only limitation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=1h4m49s).
- **Investigate better buyers and test new content.** Cohen asks about existing customers with more servers and reasons to own infrastructure beyond saving money. Orenstein proposes trying different content formats around current development workflows rather than repeating formerly successful tutorials. His comments about recommendation algorithms are an interpretation, not an audited explanation of declining traffic. The proposed path from trusted education to product customers remains to be tested. [Buyer discovery](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=12m0s); [Format experiments](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=37m47s); [Audience-to-product hypothesis](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=49m50s).
- **Preserve the disagreement about AI substitution.** Cohen emphasizes precise operations, timely maintenance, and customers who prefer to pay someone else to manage them. Orenstein questions how much of that remains difficult as coding tools improve and asks why an agent would select Hatchbox. Oliver reports one customer's use of its MCP integration, which demonstrates a use case without proving broader demand or defensibility. [Proposed complementary role](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=26m47s); [Reported customer use](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=28m55s); [Orenstein's qualification](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=31m0s); [Substitution disagreement](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=43m36s).

**Apply it:** Compare the expected service, the revenue that funds it, and the work the team wants to sustain. Test a coherent offer with suitable customers before interpreting weak economics as proof the entire product should close. This is an editorial application of the [closing pricing discussion](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/14/transcript#t=1h8m19s).

**Boundary:** Business figures, competitor comparisons, survey interpretations, and AI forecasts are participant claims. A proposed response time is not a guaranteed resolution time. The conversation reports no later pricing or retention results. Transcript fragments appear out of order, including 16:54 and 29:38; wording and speaker labels have not been checked against audio.
