# More or less

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/more-or-less/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen describes premium excellence, strong practical value, and minimal-cost access as distinct price-and-value positions. Each requires aligned choices about customers, features, service, staffing, and acquisition. A low price without an unusually low cost structure is fragile; a premium promise needs a specific buyer who values the difference. Practical value is a coherent strategy in its own right.

**Apply it:** Choose the promise the business can consistently deliver, then check whether operational spending and product tradeoffs support it.

**Boundary:** These are strategic archetypes, not exhaustive laws or permission for poor quality. Numerical thresholds and brand examples are illustrative; affordable products still need a clear, worthwhile job for the intended customer.
