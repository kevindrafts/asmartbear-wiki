# Tell me a story

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tell-me-a-story/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen discusses why an engaging narrative can remain persuasive even when its explanation is disputed. His reading of Gladwell and the critiques he cites leads to a marketing lesson: stories command attention more readily than abstract claims. The essay also exposes the risk of letting narrative appeal outrun evidence.

**Apply it:** Give a product story a concrete situation, problem, and consequence while separately checking the factual and causal claims it asks the audience to accept.

**Boundary:** The article's accounts of crime research and competing books are the author's argument, not settled conclusions established by this wiki. A compelling story is neither proof of causation nor permission to mislead.
