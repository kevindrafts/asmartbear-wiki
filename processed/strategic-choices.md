# Strategic choices: When both options are good

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/strategic-choices/) · Unofficial editorial note.

A strategic choice selects between genuinely attractive alternatives and accepts the unwanted consequences of the chosen one. Cohen shows how apparently independent decisions can conflict—or reinforce each other—through their downstream effects. His examples table is valuable as a map of trade-offs, not a menu of virtues every company should claim. Shared choices let teams act independently without pulling the business apart.

**Apply it:** Name the credible alternative, list the costs you accept, and test whether product, support, pricing, and distribution decisions remain coherent together.

**Boundary:** When two benefits do not conflict, take both. The choice governs actual collisions; it is not a reason to preserve an avoidable defect. A preference with a foolish opposite is probably a platitude rather than strategy.
