# E13 w/ Ruben Gamez: How to Restart Growth in a Stagnant SaaS Business

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Ruben Gamez, Michael Koper.

Nusii founder Michael Koper brings a proposal-software business whose revenue has plateaued. Cohen and Ruben Gamez examine whom it serves, what its homepage promises, and how a more specific product could reach more suitable buyers. Gamez draws on his experience with Bidsketch and SignWell. [Conversation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript).

- **Define the recurring job, not the customer's worth.** Koper explains that a freelancer can win a project and then stop needing proposal software. Cohen distinguishes that customer's success from a continuing need for Nusii. They explore agencies with recurring proposal work; Gamez pushes back on a proposed frequency that seems too low. The discussion's staff and proposal counts are rough descriptions, not validated qualification thresholds. [Successful customers who leave](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=3m53s); [Working segment definition](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=44m31s).
- **Connect the benefit to a credible mechanism.** Cohen asks how the product helps customers win work. Gamez distinguishes reassuring visibility and automated reminders from stronger personal sales follow-up, and points to speed and reduced signing friction. He also cautions that simplicity can mean interface ease, document presentation, or limited capability, and that competitors may all feel simple relative to manual work. [Follow-up qualification](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=11m4s); [Comparison with manual work](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=17m30s); [Different meanings of simplicity](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=26m21s).
- **Make specialization change the product.** Cohen challenges niche labels unsupported by capabilities for that buyer. He explores relevant portfolio evidence in agency proposals; Gamez later suggests checking whether a proposal addresses what the client said. These are feature hypotheses. Cohen connects such choices to a mission of helping customers win: it would affect research, product, and communication, and could develop after founding, but should not be adopted insincerely. [Words versus capabilities](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=34m44s); [Agency example](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=37m46s); [Mission developing later](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=1h0m4s); [Gamez's product suggestion](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=1h6m22s).
- **Keep acquisition work in the plan.** Gamez reports that a course integrating Bidsketch into an agency sales process brought customers. He warns that more distinctive software still needs more qualified prospects, which need not mean large amounts of generic traffic. Cohen distinguishes conference learning from sales and asks what would earn attendees' attention. These examples suggest mechanisms to test, not a demonstrated growth plan for Nusii. [Course partnership](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=31m53s); [Conference purpose](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=48m44s); [Closing acquisition constraint](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/13/transcript#t=1h7m31s).

**Apply it:** Describe the buyer's recurring work, identify the product behavior that makes the promise credible, and pair each product experiment with a way to reach that buyer. This is an editorial application.

**Boundary:** Business figures and customer patterns are participant reports. Suggested rules about proposal length, language, and improved close rates are illustrative, not measured findings. The book-proposal example illustrates specialization; Cohen explicitly rejects it as a recommended market. No subsequent growth outcome is reported.
