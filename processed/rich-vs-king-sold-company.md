# Rich vs. King in the Real World: Why I sold my company

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rich-vs-king-sold-company/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen explains selling Smart Bear through personal utility rather than maximum possible valuation. A dependable sum that secured his family's freedom mattered more than a chance at a larger future exit. Profitability both supported a satisfying business and made declining an acquisition possible, giving him bargaining power. His diagram represents a personal threshold where additional money changes life less dramatically.

**Apply it:** Compare a concrete offer with continued ownership using your financial needs, concentration of risk, desired work, and obligations to others. Establish those aims before negotiations.

**Boundary:** This is a retrospective explanation of a 2007 sale, especially relevant to small self-funded companies. The illustrative gamble is not a model of acquisition probabilities; historical valuation multiples and a later recession do not establish what another founder should accept.
