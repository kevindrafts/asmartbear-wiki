# Individual efficiency vs administrative efficiency

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/tension-autonomy-admin/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Individual preferences, team coordination, and company obligations can require different policies. Cohen favors autonomy by default while limiting standardization to a justified need. His examples distinguish shared formatting rules from a mandatory editor and shared reporting from identical departmental workflows. The meal-trading illustration shows why voluntary improvements and maximizing an aggregate score are different notions of efficiency.

**Apply it:** State the coordination or risk problem, standardize only what solves it, and explain who benefits and who bears a cost. Let people self-organize within those constraints.

**Boundary:** Local efficiency can worsen a system bottleneck, while global optimization can impose real losses on individuals. The framework requires explicit judgment rather than assuming either autonomy or central control always wins.
