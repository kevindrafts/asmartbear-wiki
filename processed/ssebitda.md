# SSEBITDA⁠—A steady-state profit metric for SaaS companies

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/ssebitda/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Removing all sales and marketing expense overstates underlying profitability if replacement customers are needed to offset churn. Cohen instead estimates steady-state earnings by adding those expenses back to EBITDA and subtracting the acquisition spending needed to maintain revenue. He then relates that surplus to acquisition cost to reason about self-funded growth.

**Apply it:** Separate replacement demand from expansion, use consistent periods and units, and inspect which inputs drive the result: margins, cancellation, acquisition efficiency, or intentional overhead investment.

**Boundary:** This is a management model, not cash flow or an accounting standard. It depends on stable acquisition and retention assumptions; extra growth may change them. Negative results can reflect stage-specific investment, and maintaining a competitive product still requires development.
