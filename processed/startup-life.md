# What a startup does to you. Or: A celebration of new life

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/startup-life/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen recalls becoming a parent and compares the experience with founding companies: both confronted him with demands beyond what prior reading could prepare him for. He ultimately treats the analogy as less useful than the shared experience of profound personal change. The essay values learning through commitment even when the venture itself fails.

**Apply it:** Consider whether the experience of building, including uncertainty and responsibility, has value to you apart from the hoped-for outcome.

**Boundary:** This is memoir, not evidence that extreme strain is beneficial for everyone. His account of founders who would repeat the experience reflects his own circle; it cannot establish that all founders should persist or that personal costs are reversible.
