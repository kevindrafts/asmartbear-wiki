# Impostor Syndrome: Why I felt like a fraud, and how I overcame it

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/impostor-syndrome/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen describes doubting his expertise while selling Smart Bear despite evidence that customers benefited. He connects the apparent contradiction between entrepreneurial conviction and insecurity to perfectionism: wanting to work your own way does not require believing your work is already good enough.

**Apply it:** Separate doubt that leads to learning, better work, and customer care from doubt that leads to concealment or avoidance. Look for evidence of improvement and allow yourself to be a learner.

**Boundary:** This is a personal account and a set of reframing suggestions, not a diagnosis or treatment protocol. The celebrity examples normalize uncertainty; they do not establish its cause for every reader. Keep the distinction between constructive striving and distress that disrupts ordinary functioning.
