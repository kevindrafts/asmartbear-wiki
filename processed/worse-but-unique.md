# Worse, but unique

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/worse-but-unique/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen uses an unusual chess opening to illustrate choosing a contest where your preparation matters more than conventional rankings. A startup can be weaker on many dimensions yet preferable to customers who strongly value its distinctive choice. Extreme commitment to that choice can make imitation difficult because competitors would have to abandon other commitments.

**Apply it:** Identify the customer who prefers your trade-offs and the capability you can pursue unusually well. Evaluate against that use case rather than a universal feature checklist.

**Boundary:** Differentiation needs real demand and acceptable baseline performance. The chess claim is an analogy rather than a settled opening evaluation, and rejecting conventional advice does not itself make a strategy sound.
