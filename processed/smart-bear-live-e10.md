# E10 w/ Jessica Malnik: Plateaued at six figures, but still hungry

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Jessica Malnik, Brett Lindenberg.

Mindstamp founder Brett Lindenberg wants faster growth without building a large team. Cohen and Jessica Malnik explore which buyers value interactive video enough to invest in it, and how to reach them beyond existing search traffic. [Conversation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript).

- **Separate product flexibility from audience breadth.** Customization can be the reason a narrow segment chooses a product. Cohen also questions adding a cheaper plan simply to win customers who value it less, while allowing that durable retention and upgrades could justify an entry tier. Lindenberg's observations about strong medical-training customers come from a small sample. [Customization](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=7m48s); [Pricing qualification](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=14m57s).
- **Distinguish curiosity from an active project.** A personalized demonstration cannot supply missing content, internal commitment, or implementation time. Cohen favors buyers who have already decided to use video and are choosing how. Creating the content could address a different buyer's real obstacle, but only if that obstacle is what prevents purchase. [26:22–28:54](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=26m22s).
- **Use services to establish product success.** They consider paid help creating or improving initial videos. Cohen distinguishes work that enables a lasting software relationship from work whose withdrawal would immediately lose the customer. The proposed packages and prices are exploratory. [32:31](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=32m31s).
- **Give a partner a reason to choose you.** Malnik proposes reaching organizations involved in recurring training and certifications. The product's role would be interactive delivery and records, while domain experts retain responsibility for the training and credentials. Cohen also suggests teaching people to make better interactive videos, making useful educational content a route to demand. These are potential partnerships and channels. [Partner idea](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=34m21s); [Role boundary](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=39m19s); [Teaching](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=54m18s).

**Apply it:** Before committing the next quarter, interview current, former, and prospective customers about their alternatives, implementation barriers, and reasons for choosing the product. Malnik proposes initial individual interviews during the first month; Cohen asks for explicit hypotheses and neutral questions, including whether buyers actually want content-creation help. This is an editorial application of their proposed research plan. [1:00:25–1:03:03](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/10/transcript#t=1h0m25s).

**Boundary:** The episode reports no results from the proposed plan. Business figures and customer patterns are participant reports; illustrative stories about improved learning or sales are not Mindstamp outcome evidence. The compliance examples explain a possible commercial role, not authoritative requirements. Transcript wording and speaker labels have not been checked against audio.
