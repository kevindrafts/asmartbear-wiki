# In command

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/in-command/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen defines competent leadership through awareness and response rather than an absence of trouble. A leader notices meaningful changes, articulates threats, prioritizes a few important fixes, and communicates what is changing and why. Written expectations make deviations visible; capable colleagues should improve decisions rather than wait for instructions.

**Apply it:** For each important problem, make its owner, current understanding, chosen response, and next review visible. Explain rejected requests as well as accepted ones.

**Boundary:** This is accountability under uncertainty, not a promise of control. Surprise, mistakes, and changing one's mind remain compatible with strong leadership when handled honestly and proportionately.
