# Extreme brainstorming questions to trigger new, better ideas

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/extreme-questions/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Extreme hypothetical constraints can reveal ideas that routine customer requests and incremental roadmaps miss. Cohen explores changes to price, distribution, support, resources, competitive copying, and shipping time. The point is to uncover an assumption worth changing: an impossible service model might reveal a feasible feature, while a severe deadline exposes unnecessary scope. He also asks what survives if competitors reproduce every feature.

**Apply it:** Choose one extreme, generate several responses, then translate the strongest response into a practical experiment with an explicit customer benefit.

**Boundary:** These are thought experiments, not instructions to eliminate support, ignore customers, or disregard people. Restore economic and human constraints before execution.
