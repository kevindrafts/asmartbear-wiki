# Your customers hate MVPs. Make a SLC instead.

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/slc/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen preserves the speed and learning goals of an MVP but asks the first release to finish a narrow customer job and give people a reason to enjoy it. Google Docs illustrates how limited functionality can still deliver a complete collaboration experience. Love can come from design, identity, service, or company behavior; it is not synonymous with visual polish.

**Apply it:** Reduce the promised job until you can deliver it end to end. Identify the particular experience customers will value immediately, then observe actual use.

**Boundary:** The pyramid diagrams contrast accumulating utility first with delivering utility and delight together within a smaller scope. Completeness does not require feature parity or flawless reliability. A successful small product may remain small; this is still an experiment and can fail.
