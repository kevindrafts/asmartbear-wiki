# E06 w/ Bryce Adams: Pivoting his strategy into his strengths

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Bryce Adams, Carl Alexander.

Carl Alexander's Ymir has loyal users but little new business. Cohen and Bryce Adams examine why technically successful infrastructure software can still have an incoherent offer, then develop a different direction using Alexander's teaching and implementation expertise. [Discussion](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript).

- **Examine the whole promise.** The hosts question the combination of critical infrastructure, dependence on a solo founder, and a $39 monthly price. Raising the price alone does not resolve the perceived dependency; the offer must explain how the customer will be supported and become capable. [24:58–27:54](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=24m58s).
- **Sell a capability transfer.** They propose paid setup and training followed by continuing software and education. Under this model, customers hire Alexander for his expertise and retain a team able to operate the system. This changes the role of his personal involvement rather than pretending it is absent. [40:10–43:04](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=40m10s).
- **Let market knowledge revise the target.** Adams challenges selling directly to moderately sized stores with assumed engineering teams. The discussion moves toward agencies with several developers that already want to run WordPress infrastructure on AWS and need expert help. That describes a promising target, not all acceptable customers or a proven market size. [Market correction](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=1h5m43s); [Target definition](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=1h24m49s).
- **Distinguish attention from a working sales process.** Engaged conference audiences had not produced customers. Adams urges a pause on conferences to focus on selling; Cohen favors events with a specific lead-generation or credibility purpose. Both connect the activity to the revised offer, rather than treating attendance itself as progress. [1:33:45–1:36:25](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/6/transcript#t=1h33m45s).

**Apply it:** Check whether the customer, price, support promise, and founder's strengths reinforce one another. If they conflict, test a different offer with suitable buyers before adding more channels. This is an editorial application.

**Boundary:** The suggested service packages and prices vary during brainstorming and are not validated offers. Alexander commits to action, but the episode supplies no subsequent sales results. Licensing remained an unproven alternative. Transcript wording and speaker labels have not been checked against audio.
