# Avoid blundering: 80% of a winning strategy

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/avoid-blundering/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen uses amateur chess errors to argue for addressing avoidable failure modes before searching for brilliant strategy. He distinguishes the final symptom of failure from actionable causes, and separates preventable mistakes from bad luck or knowingly accepted dependencies. Examples include neglecting customers, incoherent economics, premature scaling, and expansion before winning the initial market.

**Apply it:** Review which assumptions you could test now and which risks you knowingly accept. Investigate causes at a level where action remains useful.

**Boundary:** The headline percentage is not a measured startup success rate. The chess sample is small, and the enterprise-expansion warning expressly excludes companies built for enterprise buyers from the outset.
