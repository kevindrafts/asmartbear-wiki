# The three kinds of leverage that anchor effective strategies

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/leverage/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen distinguishes strengths that improve execution, strengths that win customer preference, and advantages that remain difficult to copy. He argues for concentrating effort where the company is unusually capable, while maintaining minimum acceptable standards elsewhere. The activity-map example shows how several mutually supporting choices—including deliberate limitations—can make a position harder to reproduce than a single feature.

**Apply it:** Explain how each strategic investment amplifies a strength or reinforces the same customer promise. Define thresholds for essential operational weaknesses.

**Boundary:** Reliability and security still require attention. Sometimes a strategic weakness must be repaired, but the cost should be explicit. Historical company examples illustrate the argument rather than certify their present advantages.
