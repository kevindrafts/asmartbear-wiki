# Sunk Costs: An invisible, pervasive peril

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/sunk-costs/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Past effort can make founders defend a feature, campaign, candidate, or prolonged sales opportunity after its future value becomes doubtful. Cohen argues that unrecoverable spending should not decide the next allocation. His examples distinguish attachment to prior work from the remaining costs and benefits of continuing it.

**Apply it:** Compare the options available from today, including transition costs and evidence, as if the past investment could no longer be rescued. Establish a reason for the next commitment beyond having already started.

**Boundary:** Abandonment is not always correct: switching costs, contractual obligations, learning, and recoverable assets still matter. Decisions about people need evidence and fair process rather than simply applying the article's confident anecdotes.
