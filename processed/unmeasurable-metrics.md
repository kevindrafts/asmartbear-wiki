# Metrics that cannot even be measured in retrospect

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/unmeasurable-metrics/) · Unofficial editorial note.

A business outcome may be important while its attribution to one activity remains unidentifiable. Cohen examines feature revenue, incremental churn improvements, culture, and one-off risks: multiple causes, delays, and noise make precise retrospective answers difficult, which also weakens forecasts. He favors observable system measures and explicit judgment over invented precision.

**Apply it:** Before promising a metric change, ask whether you could distinguish that intervention's effect afterward. Track useful leading evidence and overall outcomes without claiming a causal allocation the data cannot support.

**Boundary:** The argument is contextual, not a proof that rigorous experiments or causal inference never work. Repeated comparable events can support forecast evaluation; a single realized risk cannot establish whether its stated probability was calibrated.
