# The Pattern-Seeking Fallacy

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/pattern-seeking-fallacy/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Searching enough combinations almost guarantees finding something that looks unusual. Cohen connects selective sports statistics, apparent codes, and sliced marketing data to the same error: treating a pattern found after the search as though it came from a prespecified test. The dot and coin examples illustrate how ordinary randomness can look structured.

**Apply it:** Use exploratory patterns to propose hypotheses, then test them with fresh evidence and a defined measurement plan.

**Boundary:** The source explicitly updates its basketball example to acknowledge later challenges to the original hot-hand research. That qualification strengthens the practical warning about inference; it does not prove all observed streaks are random.
