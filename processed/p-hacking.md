# p-Hacking your A/B tests

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/p-hacking/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Repeated testing and stopping when results look favorable can turn noise into apparent wins. Cohen connects this to conversion improvements that fail to accumulate over time. He recommends planning tests, replicating positive findings, and testing an explicit theory about customer behavior rather than trying arbitrary variants. Both positive and negative results should update that theory.

**Apply it:** Record the hypothesis, outcome measure, and stopping rule before launch; confirm important gains with additional evidence.

**Boundary:** A significance threshold is not the probability that a particular conclusion is true. The essay's headline false-positive rate and medical examples are not universal statistical or regulatory rules; large observed effects can also be noisy in small samples.
