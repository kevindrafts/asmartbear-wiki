# E11 w/ Mariya Delano: How to Fix a SaaS Trial-to-Paid Conversion Problem

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Mariya Delano, Jane Portman, Benedikt Deicke.

Userlist's founders describe an email platform aimed at advanced SaaS marketers, but many customers use only basic capabilities. Cohen and Mariya Delano examine customer fit, implementation, and whether value is visible inside the buyer's organization. [Conversation](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript).

- **Diagnose the specific loss.** Deicke cautions against generalizing from a small number of recent price-related cancellations; one involved the departure of the person running the tool. Cohen proposes making results visible to more than one champion. Delano suggests helping users share recognizable reports with their managers. These are possible retention mechanisms, not demonstrated fixes. [12:51–19:18](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=12m51s).
- **Treat payment and implementation as separate milestones.** Portman estimates that roughly half of retained paying customers use the product as intended. Delano proposes showing what a connected data source would unlock; Cohen links that expressed interest to timely implementation help. They also explore paid onboarding, with Delano allowing a waiver for customers who already complete setup or more advanced help for those who need it. [Paying-customer estimate](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=20m9s); [Contextual help](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=22m43s); [Onboarding alternatives](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=25m28s).
- **Define implementation capacity rather than infer it from size.** Cohen describes buyers who understand the workflows and can secure the resources to implement them. Delano's example of a small company buying substantial consulting illustrates why headcount alone is insufficient. Her example does not establish Userlist's customers' budgets. [Buyer requirements](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=28m52s); [Delano's example](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=33m56s).
- **Realign the business before changing a pricing number.** Cohen argues that acquisition and pricing have lagged behind the product's evolving audience. The founders also identify reporting gaps, so this is not solely a marketing problem. His closing recommendation is to interview the intended buyers about value and charging units, then test an onboarding offer. [Reporting qualification](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=55m14s); [Pricing research](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/11/transcript#t=59m2s).

**Apply it:** Separate unsuitable buyers, buyers who never implement, and customers whose value becomes invisible after personnel changes. Test the corresponding intervention against that group rather than treating every cancellation as a price objection. This is an editorial application.

**Boundary:** Suggested onboarding fees vary and are not validated prices. Competitor comparisons are disputed during the conversation; billing units also differ. The proposed sales script's failure-rate figure is illustrative, not a measured Userlist result. No subsequent conversion or retention outcomes are reported.
