# High-concept pitches are not your friend

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/high-concept-pitch/) · Unofficial editorial note.

A familiar-product analogy is brief but carries associations the founder may not intend. Cohen's hypothetical portable projector inherits both desirable and undesirable meanings when described through the iPhone. A specific positioning sentence can retain the benefit while avoiding that ambiguity.

**Apply it:** Ask a listener what an analogy implies, then replace it with a clear description of the customer benefit if the inferred promises diverge.

**Boundary:** The product and phone examples are historical and illustrative. Cohen favors brevity, but treats clarity as the constraint; he does not argue that longer pitches are automatically better.
