# The “errors” that mean you’re doing it right

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/good-problems-to-have/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Some unpleasant consequences are evidence of a sensible tradeoff, rather than proof of a bad decision. Cohen includes restoring a culled backlog item, rebuilding infrastructure after major growth, and revising a strategy when execution reveals an error. Avoiding every such outcome would require expensive caution or prevent learning. The response still matters: fix the issue and explain the tradeoff.

**Apply it:** Ask whether a problem came from negligence, bad luck, or an intentionally accepted cost of a useful policy.

**Boundary:** The essay explicitly limits waiting until support or sales breaks to the early learning stage. At scale, failing to plan capacity is mismanagement; repeated hiring mistakes likewise demand process repair.
