# “Authentic” is dead. And so is “is dead.”

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/authentic-is-dead/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen objects to exhausted marketing language because it asks buyers to accept claims without evidence. He favors concrete descriptions, demonstrations, customer testimony, and candid acknowledgment of a product’s limits. His own demo introduction illustrates how admitting what software cannot fix can make narrower claims more credible.

**Apply it:** Replace each generic adjective with an observable outcome, a demonstration, or relevant customer evidence.

**Boundary:** He endorses the underlying behaviors, including authenticity and service, while criticizing their empty assertion. A familiar phrase can still work when the company demonstrably fulfills it; the numerical examples are illustrative copy, not figures to reuse.
