# E04 w/ Dr. Sherry Walling: He built a great business, but it doesn't feel like it

[Read the official transcript](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript) · [Episode and audio](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4) · Unofficial editorial note.

Participants: Jason Cohen, Dr. Sherry Walling, Patrik Inzinger.

Patrik Inzinger describes a stable, profitable Appointmed business while questioning what comes next. Sherry Walling and Cohen explore recognition, personal purpose, and future ambition without treating a healthy company as a failure. [Conversation, from 07:40](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript).

- **Choose whose feedback deserves weight.** Walling suggests seeking people who understand the company and can give specific, honest assessments. Recognition can help, but should not be the founder's only source of motivation. This differs from measuring the business against attention-grabbing funding announcements. [38:26–39:02 and 1:00:29–1:02:03](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript).
- **Separate personal purpose from company direction.** Walling distinguishes Inzinger's contribution as a person from continuing a healthy business. Cohen distinguishes a broader desired change from the company's role in it; a technical rewrite or excellent support does not by itself answer the broader purpose question. [1:17:44–1:26:23](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript).
- **Gather observations before declaring an identity.** Walling proposes recording specific energizing and draining moments over time, then looking for themes. Trusted people can add an outside view of when the founder seems most engaged and what help they seek from him. These are reflection exercises, not clinical conclusions about Inzinger. [1:35:27–1:37:58 and 1:50:28–1:52:29](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript).
- **Explore ambition without committing to it.** Cohen proposes working backward from a demanding future to identify the changes it would require. The exercise might reveal an attractive direction, a smaller expansion consistent with the team's preferences, or good reasons to keep the present model. Execution feasibility still needs separate examination. [1:54:29–2:01:26](https://smartbearlive.transistor.fm/4/transcript).

**Apply it:** Write separate questions for the business's next objective and your own next chapter. Use observed experience, trusted feedback, and small trials to refine both. This is an editorial application of the discussion.

**Boundary:** These are suggestions made in a founder conversation, not evidence that a particular life or growth plan worked.
