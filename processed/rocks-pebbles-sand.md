# Rocks, Pebbles, Sand: How to implement in practice

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/rocks-pebbles-sand/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen expands the familiar jar analogy into different decision rules for different work. Large strategic commitments need exceptional absolute impact and deliberate scrutiny; medium improvements need credible near-term benefit relative to effort; small maintenance and polish need inexpensive coordination. His tables show why one scoring process fails: estimation error compounds in a ratio, while individually unmeasurable fixes still create collective quality.

**Apply it:** Protect progress on a major outcome while retaining capacity for urgent obligations, tactical improvements, and upkeep. Diagnose persistent interruption as a capacity or operating problem.

**Boundary:** Size alone does not determine strategic value. The categories and durations are guidance, not mandatory sprint quotas; balance matters over time, and completed features often require follow-through before a new initiative starts.
