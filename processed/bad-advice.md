# Distinguishing constructive criticism from bad business advice

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/bad-advice/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen contrasts a prestigious executive’s proposal to remake Smart Bear with a mentor’s questions about whether that arrangement served the business. Advice needs to fit the company’s customers, resources, goals, and founder—not merely the adviser’s résumé.

**Apply it:** Ask for the reasoning, an implementable experiment, and evidence that would show whether the recommendation works. Seek advisers who challenge assumptions through questions.

**Boundary:** Discomfort may signal either a mismatch or an unwelcome truth. Personal fit should not become an excuse to avoid necessary work, and conventional advice is neither automatically right nor automatically wrong.
