# The fundamental forces of scale

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/scale/) · Unofficial editorial note.

Cohen attributes scaling difficulties to frequent exceptions and interactions too complex for any individual to understand fully. Redundant teams and systems cost more per unit but preserve continuity; coordinated launches and hiring require greater predictability; new initiatives must clear a larger materiality threshold. His infrastructure example shows how redundancy also introduces components and dependencies, not merely extra copies.

**Apply it:** Identify where a once-effective informal practice now creates fragility, then add the expertise, documentation, coordination, or capacity needed for continuity. Preserve core values while changing operating methods.

**Boundary:** Slower execution is not automatically waste, and bureaucracy is not automatically justified. The source flags correlated failures in its simplified reliability arithmetic. Statements about early-stage disaster planning are contextual, not permission to ignore obligations to customers.
