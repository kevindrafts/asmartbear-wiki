# The Important Thing⁠—powerful enough to override all your deficiencies

[Read Jason Cohen’s original](https://longform.asmartbear.com/the-important-thing/) · Unofficial editorial note.

A young product can win despite many shortcomings when one benefit matters enough for a particular customer to tolerate the rest. Cohen uses early iPhone and Heroku experiences, along with attachment to a company's philosophy, to show that this benefit can be functional or emotional. Trying to repair every weakness may prevent excellence anywhere.

**Apply it:** Identify the outcome or experience customers actively choose you for, and test which limitations those same customers will accept. Concentrate effort on making that reason compelling.

**Boundary:** The examples are retrospective explanations, not proof that one strength always compensates for deficiencies. Some requirements remain disqualifying, and competitors, technology, and customer expectations change.
